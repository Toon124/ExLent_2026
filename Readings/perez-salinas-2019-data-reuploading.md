# Data Re-uploading for a Universal Quantum Classifier

**Authors:** Pérez-Salinas, Cervera-Lierta, Gil-Fuster, Latorre
**Year:** 2019
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02085

## Summary
The Data Re-uploading for a universal quantum classifier paper explores how single or few-qubit quantum circuits can act as universal classifiers. The paper’s introduction immediately highlights the importance of quantum circuits that utilize minimal resources. It states that even if these circuits give no quantum advantage, they could still be useful segments of larger circuits. Though there are various methods to create a quantum classifier, the paper focuses on using a variational or parameterized circuit to explore the question “what is the lower limit of quantum resources needed to achieve a given computation?” 
A single qubit can perform one rotation once data has been loaded onto it. This single rotation isn’t enough to separate non-trivial patterns in  the data. Data re-uploading allows the use of classical data to work around this and the limitations imposed by the no-cloning theorem. The classical data is fed into the circuit many times and allows the circuit to find complex relationships in the data. The paper applies this approach to multiple qubits, which reveals a trade-off. When there are more entangled qubits the number of re-uploads needed is reduced, so qubits and data re-uploading act as interchangeable resources. This supports the claim that data re-uploading can be used to separate complex patterns in the data without more qubits.
The results of the paper show that a single-qubit classifier using data re-uploading is a viable universal classifier, achieving over 90% accuracy across all the problems it was tested on. It performed comparably and even better on some problems than classical methods. The results also find that adding more qubits and entangling them allows for greater accuracy while reducing the need for as many layers, emphasizing the relationship between data re-uploading and the number of qubits.
In conclusion, the paper shows that data re-uploading allows even a single qubit to act as a universal classifier by feeding the classical data into the circuit multiple times, which allows it to bypass the limits of few-qubit circuits. Although the paper only tests abstract problems like shapes, this approach shows promise for real-world data such as biomedical datasets. Medical data like tumor measurements often have non-linear boundaries between classes, which is precisely what data re-uploading is being designed to capture. The ability to do this with few qubits allows for a practical way to apply quantum classification to real problems without needing large quantum hardware, making it more realistic for near-future applications.
